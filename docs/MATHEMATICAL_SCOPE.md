# Mathematical scope and future work

The book's central question is whether distinguishability makes a statistical geometry necessary rather than arbitrary. The finite theory supplies a precise answer, not a universal one.

## Statements established in the working edition

- **Čencov uniqueness:** a compatible family of metrics on interiors of finite probability simplexes, invariant under congruent stochastic embeddings admitting stochastic recovery, is a common positive multiple of the Fisher metric. Mere coordinate invariance is too weak.
- **Data processing:** a stochastic map contracts Fisher quadratic forms; the proof uses conditional expectation of scores. Reversible statistical refinements preserve them.
- **Divergence-induced structure:** the diagonal second/third derivatives of an appropriate smooth contrast define a metric and dual affine connections, with Bregman/KL identities in the specified exponential-family context.
- **Projection geometry:** the Bregman three-point identity is exact. The Pythagorean inequality is the general convex-projection statement; equality requires the appropriate affine/interior hypotheses.
- **Learning flows:** natural gradients are geometrically defined steepest descent and are invariant under smooth reparameterization where the metric is nondegenerate.

## Do not overgeneralize

- A stochastic contraction is not generally an isometry.
- A Riemannian Fisher geodesic is not in general a mixture or exponential straight line.
- Dual affine flatness is not equivalent to zero Riemannian curvature.
- KL divergence is directional and not the Fisher distance.
- Finite-simplex uniqueness does not automatically extend to arbitrary singular, infinite-dimensional, Wasserstein, or quantum models.
